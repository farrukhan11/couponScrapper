NI — daily, steady. A knotted cord on the wrist, quiet and unhurried, there through the ordinary days.

### Chöni Phuntsok

Above four thousand metres the air is thin, the sun fierce, the day-to-night swing past twenty degrees. Whatever survives grows slowly, snow lotus waits five or six springs to flower, spikenard roots three years before it heals, drawing sun, snow and minerals into itself, fierce and pure. Before gathering, one turns to the mountain and gives quiet thanks, taking only enough, never the root. Here, medicine is not used, it is asked for.  
  
ME — asked, not taken. A quiet thanks to the mountain, taking only enough: these herbs are asked for, never seized.

### Benglo

Tibetan Thokcha is a star fallen onto the plateau. The maker calls himself not a miner but a messenger for the earth, you take only what it agrees to release. Following the lima copper formula handed down from Khyentse Wangpo more than two hundred years ago, the copper and sky-iron are annealed, hammered and shaped over the forge. The hardest metal, subdued by fire and force, becomes something worn close, to guard you.  
  
HUM — a star, a shield. Tibetan Thokcha began as a fallen star; through fire and hammer it becomes something that guards you.
